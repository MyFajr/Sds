# My Daily Routine
<small>My day, everyday habits and the Present Simple · A1 · General American</small>

> [!abstract] Lesson overview
> In this lesson, you will learn to talk about your everyday routine, understand common time expressions, and use the **Present Simple** in affirmative sentences, negatives and questions.
>
> **В этом уроке:** распорядок дня, повседневные действия, время и базовая грамматика Present Simple.

<span class="st-le">A daily routine is a group of things you do every day or almost every day. You can use simple English to describe your morning, your work or study day, and your evening.</span>

---

## 1. Learning goals

By the end of this lesson, you can:

- <span class="st-fa">name common daily activities</span>;
- <span class="st-fa">say what time you do things</span>;
- <span class="st-fa">describe habits with the Present Simple</span>;
- <span class="st-fa">ask another person about their routine</span>.

<span class="st-mi">The key idea: use the Present Simple to talk about habits, routines and things that are generally true.</span>

<span class="st-ac">You do not need long or complicated sentences. A short, clear sentence is a good sentence.</span>

<span class="st-so">Try to notice the same words several times in different examples. Repetition helps you remember them.</span>

## 3. Grammar: the Present Simple

We use the <span class="st-te">Present Simple</span> for habits, routines and repeated actions.

> [!tip] Think of a routine
> If something happens every day, every week or regularly, the Present Simple is often the right tense.

### 3.1 Affirmative sentences

The basic structure is:

<span class="st-im">Subject + verb + the rest of the sentence.</span>

| Subject | Verb | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I / You / We / They | work | I work every day. |
| He / She / It | works | She works every day. |

**Remember:** with **he**, **she** and **it**, we usually add **-s** or **-es** to the verb.

- I <span class="st-ex">get up</span> at seven.
- She <span class="st-ex">gets up</span> at seven.
- They <span class="st-ex">study</span> English.
- He <span class="st-ex">studies</span> English.

> [!warning] Common mistake
> ❌ She get up at seven.
>
> ✅ She gets up at seven.
>
> Не забывай окончание **-s / -es** после he, she, it.

### 3.2 Negative sentences

Use **do not (don't)** or **does not (doesn't)** + the base form of the verb.

| Subject | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I / You / We / They | don't + verb | I don't work on Sunday. |
| He / She / It | doesn't + verb | He doesn't work on Sunday. |

<span class="st-wa">After doesn't, do not add -s to the main verb: “She doesn't work”, not “She doesn't works”.</span>

### 3.3 Questions

Use **Do** or **Does** at the beginning.

- **Do** you study English every day?
- **Does** he work in the morning?
- **What time do** you get up?
- **When does** she go to bed?

Short answers:

- Yes, I do. / No, I don't.
- Yes, she does. / No, she doesn't.

<span class="st-qu">What time do you usually wake up?</span>

Take a moment and answer aloud before you continue.

## 4. Time expressions and frequency

These words tell us when or how often something happens.

| Expression | Значение | Example |
|---|---|---|
| every day | каждый день | I study every day. |
| in the morning | утром | I exercise in the morning. |
| in the afternoon | днём | I work in the afternoon. |
| in the evening | вечером | I read in the evening. |
| at night | ночью | I sleep at night. |
| on weekdays | по будням | I work on weekdays. |
| on weekends | по выходным | I relax on weekends. |

### Adverbs of frequency

These words describe how often we do something.

| Adverb | Meaning |
|---|---|
| <span class="st-fo">always</span> | всегда |
| <span class="st-fo">usually</span> | обычно |
| <span class="st-fo">often</span> | часто |
| <span class="st-fo">sometimes</span> | иногда |
| <span class="st-fo">rarely</span> | редко |
| <span class="st-fo">never</span> | никогда |

<span class="st-de">Frequency adverbs usually come before the main verb: “I usually have breakfast at home.” With the verb “to be”, they usually come after it: “I am usually busy.”</span>

## 5. Read: Alex's typical day

Read the short text. Notice the verbs and time expressions.

> [!example] A day in Alex's life
> Alex is a student. He **wakes up** at 7:00 a.m. He **gets up**, brushes his teeth and takes a shower. He usually **has breakfast** at 7:30.
>
> At 8:00, he **goes to class**. He studies English and mathematics. He **has lunch** at 1:00 p.m.
>
> In the afternoon, Alex comes home and does his homework. In the evening, he sometimes watches a film or reads a book. He **goes to bed** at 11:00 p.m.

### Check your understanding

1. What time does Alex wake up?
2. What does he do after breakfast?
3. What subjects does he study?
4. What does he sometimes do in the evening?
5. What time does he go to bed?

<span class="st-qt">“He usually has breakfast at 7:30.”</span>

This sentence contains a frequency adverb, a verb in the third-person form, and a time expression.

## 6. A short dialogue

**Mia:** What time do you get up?

**Ben:** I usually get up at seven. How about you?

**Mia:** I get up at half past seven. Do you have breakfast at home?

**Ben:** Yes, I do. I usually have eggs and coffee.

**Mia:** Do you work or study?

**Ben:** I study. I have English classes in the morning.

> [!info] Useful conversation phrases
> - **How about you?** — А ты?
> - **What time…?** — Во сколько…?
> - **I usually…** — Я обычно…
> - **Not every day.** — Не каждый день.

## 9. Quick review

- <span class="st-su">Present Simple describes habits, routines and general facts.</span>
- Use the base verb with **I / you / we / they**.
- Add **-s / -es** with **he / she / it** in affirmative sentences.
- Use **don't / doesn't** to make negatives.
- Use **do / does** to ask questions.

<span class="st-co">The most important pattern to remember: “I work” — “She works” — “She doesn't work” — “Does she work?”</span>

## 11. One-minute speaking challenge

Speak for one minute about your daily routine. Use at least five verbs from the vocabulary section and two frequency adverbs.

You can start like this:

> I usually wake up at… Then I… In the morning, I… In the afternoon, I… In the evening, I… I go to bed at…

<span class="st-im">Speaking tip: do not stop after every mistake. Finish your thought first. You can correct the sentence afterwards.</span>
